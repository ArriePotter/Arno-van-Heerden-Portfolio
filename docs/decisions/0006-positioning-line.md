# 0006: Positioning: "Product Designer who proves the why"

- **Date:** 2026-10-18
- **Status:** Accepted
- **Department:** Product strategy / Content design
- **Phase:** Define
- **Decided by:** Arno

## Context

Reviewers judge a page in seconds and decide to stay or leave within about 10 s (`docs/research/portfolio/desk/evidence-ledger.md`). The first screen must say the role and the differentiator. The brief (§7) had two candidates: A, "Product Designer who proves the why", and B, "Product Designer driven by why things work".

## Options considered

1. **A, "who proves the why".** It's bold and claims open ground, but it's a promise every case study must keep.
2. **B, "driven by why things work".** It's safer, but it describes a motive rather than a proof.

## Decision

> **Product Designer who proves the why.**
> I research, design and ship products end to end, with AI-assisted code.

The support line shows the full process and the differentiator (shipping with AI-assisted code), without tying Arno to one platform. Seniority isn't stated on the hero; it goes on the About page and the CV.

## Evidence

- Teardown: three of the four sites scored 0/5 on "Evidence of why" (`docs/research/portfolio/teardown-insights.md`).
- NN/g survey of 204 hiring managers: they want the process and the reasoning, not just the final screens.
- Decision 0001: AI-assisted design-to-code is the differentiator.
- *Judgement:* lead with what you do, not with what you lack.

## Consequences

- **Risk #3 stays open:** "proves" must be kept. Every key decision on the site needs a visible reason and a source. Test it in the prototype review (decision 0004).
- The case-study template must have a place for evidence on every decision.

## Case-study note

I chose the bolder line because the research showed that the "why" is the gap in most portfolios, and I built the site so that it has to keep that promise.
