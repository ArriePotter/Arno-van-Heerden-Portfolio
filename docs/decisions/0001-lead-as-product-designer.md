# 0001: Lead as Product Designer, with AI-assisted design-to-code as the differentiator

- **Date:** 2026-10-03
- **Status:** Accepted
- **Department:** Product strategy
- **Phase:** Brief
- **Decided by:** Arno

## Context

The portfolio needs one primary role to sell, because the headline, case studies and interview prep all depend on it. There were two candidates: "Design Engineer (AI-fluent)", from Arno's first brief answers, and "Product Designer", from the career roadmap. Arno ships web and iOS products with AI assistance but does not hand-write code. One of Arno's stated fears is that the interview won't back up the portfolio.

## Options considered

1. **Lead as Design Engineer.**
   - Pros: matches the craft-plus-code ambition.
   - Cons: most Design Engineer and UX Engineer roles expect years of hand-written engineering (career roadmap research, Oct 2026), so live-coding interviews would expose the gap.
2. **Lead as Product Designer, with code as the differentiator.**
   - Pros: matches what the primary targets hire for (Isoflow, Yoco, Revolut), and everything shown can be defended in an interview.
   - Cons: some pure Design Engineer roles become stretch applications.
3. **A hybrid title** ("Designer & Engineer").
   - Cons: unclear, and it fails the reader's 10-second "what is this person?" check.

## Decision

Lead with **Product Designer**. The differentiator is that Arno **bridges Figma and code with AI-assisted engineering**: Arno designs in Figma and ships it to production. This must be visible on the site, not buried. Design Engineer roles stay as stretch targets.

## Evidence

- Career roadmap research (Oct 2026): Apple and Google design-engineering roles ask for 4–5+ years of software engineering.
- Tom Scott, *Design hiring FAQ 2026* (`docs/sources.md`): companies value designers who show "AI fluency" and "own more of the frontend", but they flag any gap between what a candidate claims and what they can demonstrate.
- Non-negotiable #7 (be honest about AI) and Arno's interview-fear answer (brief Q7).

## Consequences

- Headline, CV and LinkedIn use "Product Designer".
- The AI-assisted build is shown openly: each case study has a "where AI helped, where it failed" section.
- Revisit once Arno can hand-write production front-end code.

## Case-study note

"I chose a title I could defend in any interview, and made the AI-assisted build part of the story instead of hiding it."
