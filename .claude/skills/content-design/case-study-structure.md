# Case-study structure

The target is a 5-minute read that works as a 30-second skim. The headings alone should tell the story.

0. **Title = the outcome.** Write "Helping first-time budgeters set up a budget in under a minute", not "Finanza redesign".
1. **Summary card:** role (what *Arno* owned), timeline, team, tools, and **one outcome number or evidence line**. Add the live link, TestFlight or repo, and the Figma file link when it's shareable.
2. **Problem & constraints:** who struggled, with what, and why it mattered (business + user). Include real constraints such as time, platform and data.
3. **Research:** the methods and sample size ("6 interviews, 5 usability tests"), and the 3 key insights, each with a verbatim quote and frequency.
4. **Synthesis:** job stories, flows or a journey map as **images**, not text walls.
5. **Exploration:** the 2–3 directions and **why each rejected one was killed**. This is where judgement shows.
6. **Final design:** hero screens, key flows, and the states (empty/error/loading). Use annotated callouts that explain decisions.
7. **System:** the tokens and components it's built from, and how they scale.
8. **Build:** how it shipped, 1–2 code or token snippets, and **where AI helped and where it failed** (with honesty).
9. **Validation:** before/after evidence such as task success, time on task, SUS or reviewer feedback. Report misses too.
10. **Reflection:** what Arno would do next and what was learned.

Rules:

- Every claim of improvement shows its evidence.
- Every image has a caption that says *what to notice*.
- Use a **decision callout** pattern: "Decision → Why → Evidence" (pull these from `docs/decisions/`).
- Embedded Figma links must be view-only and checked for stray WIP and personal data before sharing.
